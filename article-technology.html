Is Technology Making Our Lives Better?

Technology has transformed the way we work, communicate and learn. But does having more technology necessarily mean having a better life?

By TOUGHanswers August 24, 2026 7 min read

Technology is everywhere. It is in our phones, homes, schools, workplaces, vehicles and even the way we interact with one another.

In a matter of decades, technology has changed many things that previous generations could hardly imagine. We can communicate across continents instantly, access enormous amounts of information and perform tasks that once required hours or days.

But one important question remains:

"Does technological progress always mean human progress?"

Technology has made life more convenient

Consider how quickly we can communicate today. A message can travel across the world almost instantly. Online banking allows people to manage money without visiting a bank. Online learning gives students access to educational resources from almost anywhere.

These are significant improvements.

But convenience has a price

The same technology that saves time can also consume it. Social media, entertainment platforms and constant notifications can make it difficult for people to disconnect.

A tool designed to make communication easier can sometimes make meaningful conversation more difficult.

Are we becoming too dependent?

Imagine losing your smartphone for an entire day. Could you remember the phone numbers you need? Could you navigate without GPS? Could you work without your usual applications?

Technology becomes a problem when we stop controlling our tools and allow our tools to control us.

Technology should serve people

The goal should not be to reject technology. Technology can improve healthcare, education, communication, business and countless other areas of life.

The challenge is learning how to use it intentionally.

The real question

Instead of asking whether technology is completely good or completely bad, perhaps we should ask a better question:

"Am I using technology, or is technology using me?"

That question changes the conversation. It puts responsibility back in our hands.

TOUGHANSWER:

Technology can make our lives better, but technology itself cannot determine what a better life means. We still need wisdom, discipline and purpose to use powerful tools well.
